Air pressure

—
What it means

hPa EST. is the estimated air pressure at your altitude: the weight of the air above you.

1 hPa (hectopascal) = 100 Pa = 1 millibar. Standard sea-level pressure is 1013.25 hPa, or 29.92 inches of mercury.

How it is estimated

The app converts your GPS altitude h (metres) into pressure with the International Standard Atmosphere formula:

P = 1013.25 × (1 − 2.25577×10⁻⁵·h)5.25588 hPa

It is a calculation, not a reading from the phone’s barometer: a web app cannot access that sensor. Real pressure also rises and falls with the weather, so the true value can differ by a few percent.

Good to know
  • Water boils at a lower temperature when pressure is lower.
  • Because water boils cooler, food cooked in boiling water needs more time at altitude. Oven temperatures are not affected.
  • Half of the air molecules in the atmosphere lie below about 5.6 km (18,000 ft), so pressure there is about half of sea level.
  • Near sea level the pressure drops by about 12 hPa for every 100 m you climb (from the formula above). That is why a 1 hPa change in the weather shifts a barometric altimeter by about 8 m.
  • Sealed bags and bottles packed at low altitude swell as you go up: the air inside keeps its original pressure while the air outside drops (Boyle’s law).
Sources

NOAA JetStream, “Air Pressure”.
USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, “High Altitude Cooking”.
NIST Chemistry WebBook, water vapour pressure (Antoine equation parameters).
International Standard Atmosphere (ICAO).

O₂ vs sea level

—
What it means

At your current altitude, each breath holds this share of the oxygen a breath holds at sea level.

How it is estimated

The app converts your GPS altitude h (metres) into air pressure with the International Standard Atmosphere formula, then compares it with sea-level pressure:

P = 1013.25 × (1 − 2.25577×10⁻⁵·h)5.25588 hPa
O₂ EST. = P ÷ 1013.25

It is a calculation, not a sensor reading. Weather moves the real air pressure up or down by a few percent, and GPS altitude has its own error (see V-ACC).

Good to know
  • Air is about 21% oxygen at every altitude, in Tibet as in Shanghai. What drops is air pressure: the air is thinner, so each breath contains fewer oxygen molecules.
  • O₂ EST. describes the air, not your body. Blood oxygen saturation (SpO₂) is measured with a pulse oximeter or a watch.
  • For most people, a normal SpO₂ reading is 95–100%. Readings are often lower at high elevation.
  • SpO₂ falls as altitude rises, especially above about 2,500 m, in a study of 6,289 healthy, acclimatised people at 15 locations from sea level up to the highest permanent human settlement in the Andes.
  • For home oximeter use, Cleveland Clinic advises calling a healthcare provider at 92% or lower and going to an emergency room at 88% or lower. This app does not measure SpO₂ and is not a medical device.
Sources

Cleveland Clinic, “Blood Oxygen Level”.
Rojas-Camayo J. et al., Thorax 2018;73(8), “Reference values for oxygen saturation from sea level to the highest human habitation in the Andes in acclimatised persons”.
APEX / altitude.org, “High Altitude”.
